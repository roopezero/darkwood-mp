## P01. Bumped and pushed objects don't stay in sync

- **Status:** fixed in 2a33daa (the test in d90243e). Triage found it partially implemented: pushes were already shared: `DragSync.WatchPushes` (`src/DarkwoodMP/World/DragSync.cs`) watches every loose thing (an Item with a movable rigidbody) within 600 of each game's player, and sends where one comes to rest; a door leaf walked into is `DoorPush` (`src/DarkwoodMP/World/DoorLeaf.cs`). But no test had ever pushed anything with a player's body: `map:knock` and `sweep-push-door` set velocities or walk by putting the player in place frame by frame, and only with one client, on localhost (no lag).
- **The test:** `tools/tests/map4_push.py` (steps in `src/DarkwoodMP/World/MapScenarioPush.cs`), in `map4` with three client games. Players push things with their bodies through the physics, then another player bumps the same thing where their game has it, and all four games' `items`, `loose` (turn) and `doors` sections are compared after each. It covers: a client's bump and another client's bump after it, the host's bump and a client's after it, leaning on a thing (pressed for 5 s after it moved), a hit from 150 away, two pushes right after each other, a push while another client's copy is switched off by its culling (that client then comes back and bumps it), an open door's leaf walked through by a client and by the host, and a house far from the host where things are named by where they stand. `DWMP_LAG_MS=N` (new, `Net/Session.cs`) holds every message that arrives N ms in every game, as an internet link does.
- **Findings:**
  1. On localhost, every body push, lean, re-bump, door leaf and switched-off copy matched in all four games (first run, before any fix).
  2. **A hit's push from further than 120 was undone on the client** (`ps-hit`: the stool hit from 142 didn't move). A shot pushes what it hits from as far as the gun reaches (`Player` shot code: `getHit`, then `AddForceAtPosition`), but a client counts a move as its player's only within 120 of its player (`PushTouch`), so it put the thing back; the host gets the hit (ItemHit) but not its push, so nothing moved anywhere. Fixed: our player's hit on a thing (`ItemHitPatch` → `DragSync.Hit`) makes its moves for 3 s our player's push.
  3. **With lag, the host's echo of a client's push pulled the thing back under the pusher.** The host passes every rest position back to its mover too. A client that sent a rest (also the small rest after walking up to a thing) and pushed on got its old position back 2 × lag later: the thing jumped back and was held still for 0.25 s under the player. Fixed: a client skips the echo of a position it sent itself (`DragSync.OurEcho`); anything else said about the thing (the host's own word, a refusal, another player's move) is taken, and so are the echoes after it, so every game still ends with the host's last word.
  4. **Others saw a pushed or hit thing jump to where it stopped** (seen by the user watching a test run). Only the rest position was sent. Fixed: each game checks its loose things ten times a second (new ones are still looked for four times a second) and sends one that's still sliding as a drag's moves (unreliably); the host passes on a client's without a drag claim when the client is at it and it stays within reach (`TakeMove`), and the others glide the last bit to the rest (at most 0.6 s; 300 or more is a jump). `host-drag`'s case 6 now checks that a claimless move to far away is dropped and one beside the thing is passed on.
  5. **A door's leaf pushed past 105 degrees** (seen in the lagged run: the host's leaf at -125) was put at 105 in the other games, and refused from a client over 110. Some doors have their hinge limit switched off (`Door.disableLimit`). Fixed: the clamp is the leaf's own hinge limit (`DoorLeaf.MaxTurn`). A new `leaves` dump section compares each open leaf's angle (within 4 degrees, DoorPush's `MinChange`).
  6. **Door leaves jumped too, and a slow turn was never sent** (the first seen by the user; the second in a lagged run, the host's leaf at -65 and every client's at -72 after a player's body was eased gently against it). Fixed (`DoorPush`): a swinging leaf's angle is streamed and the others turn towards it each frame, the pusher skipping its echoes; a leaf more than 4 degrees from where it's shared is followed to its rest however slowly it got there; the host sends any rest of its own leaf (its enemies' pushes too), and a client swings a turn that wasn't its player's back to the shared angle.
- **Not found:** the exact case in the report (another player still sees the thing where it was, and their bump puts it back) didn't happen in any run: on localhost and with 120 ms of lag, each push reached the host and both other clients, also a client whose copy was switched off far away, and the next player's bump started from there. Findings 2 and 3 can look like it (a thing hit from afar stays put for the others; under lag the pusher's thing jumps back to where it last rested). If it's still seen after these fixes, the log of each game (with `--dwmp-debug-world`) shows `pushed ... sent where it is` in the pusher's and `NAME is moving ...` in the others'.
- **Verification (final build):** `DWMP_LAG_MS=120 MAP_SCENARIO=map4_push tools/run-test.sh map4` PASS (482 s) and the same without lag PASS: every step done and every compare the same in all four games, no exceptions, saves unchanged. `host-drag` PASS, `map:sweep` PASS (door leaves), `map:knock` PASS (pushed dropped items). Manual: see the checklist.
- **Related:** P02 (players' bodies).

## P02. Soft collision between players

- **Status:** fixed in 1a832ff. Triage found it not implemented: players' bodies don't collide at all: the puppets have no solid colliders (`Sync/RemotePlayer.cs`, `EnsureRoot`), and on the host the AI's stand-ins ignore the host's player (`Combat/NetPlayerProxy.cs`).
- **Fix:** `Sync/PlayerSeparation.cs`. Each game eases only its own player out of every other player's puppet nearer than two body widths, faster the deeper they overlap (120 units a second at the centre, nothing at the edge), through the player's rigidbody (walls still stop it). Both games do it, so each player takes half the way out. Walking into someone only slows you down, so nobody can block a doorway. Setting `General.SoftPlayerCollision` (on by default). No protocol change: it moves only the local player, which is sent as usual.
- **Verification:** `ps-overlap` in `map4_push.py` (a player put right on another's puppet must be eased at least 3/4 of the way apart within 4 s, and not pushed more than three times that far): c1 onto the host, c2 onto c1 and the host onto c1 were 33-37 apart (of 44) after 0.5-0.6 s, with and without lag. Manual: see the checklist.
- **Size:** S.

## P03. Deterministic physics and RNG, for rollback netcode

- **Status:** unclear; a design decision, not started.
- **Evidence:** the mod is host-authoritative with client-side prediction and server reconciliation (`CLAUDE.md`, `World/Authority.cs`). Darkwood's physics is Unity's PhysX (not the same across machines and builds, and no way to save and restore the scene's physics), its logic runs in `Update` with a variable `Time.deltaTime`, and it rolls from `UnityEngine.Random` and its own `RandomNumberGenerator` throughout (`docs/MULTIPLAYER_DETERMINISM_AUDIT.md`).
- **What is possible:** (1) shared RNG: the host seeds the game's generators for the rolls the mod shares (feasible; today the host rolls and sends the result instead). (2) Rollback for one subsystem: take loose furniture, door leaves and players' bodies out of PhysX and step them in the mod, in fixed-point maths at a fixed tick (the game is top-down, so 2D shapes would do), with a few ticks of history to rewind and replay on a late input. Every game would then compute the same pushes, with no corrections. (3) Rollback for the whole game: enemy AI, animators, coroutines, A* pathfinding and scripted events run in non-deterministic Unity code that a mod can't snapshot or rewind; not realistic.
- **Proposed:** if wanted, a prototype of (2) for loose objects alone, behind a setting, measured against the current model (`map4_push` with `DWMP_LAG_MS`).
- **Size:** L (weeks): a physics step, collision shapes built from the game's colliders, a tick-based input stream, history buffers, a protocol change.

## P04. Rollback for player movement and actions

- **Status:** already implemented in the form that fits: a client does its player's action at once and the host checks it (`World/Authority.cs`); a refused one is rolled back (the host's `ObjState`, `InventoryWatch` undoing the frame's inventory change). A client moves its own player and the host checks the move (`Sync/MoveCheck.cs`, `PlayerCorrection` for one that isn't plausible). The host doesn't simulate a client's movement, so there's no host position to rewind to and replay unconfirmed inputs from: input-replay reconciliation would change nothing in normal play. Pushes follow the same pattern (P01's echo skip is the reconciliation step).
- **What the user saw:** the delay of *other* players and things on our screen: one-way latency, plus remote players drawn one update behind (`Sync/RemotePlayer.cs`, interpolation between the last two snapshots), plus 120 ms in each game from `DWMP_LAG_MS` in that run. Rollback can't remove it: their inputs haven't arrived yet.
- **Proposed:** extrapolation (dead reckoning): draw a remote player, and a thing another game is sliding, where it probably is now (last position plus velocity times the time since, capped), and blend into the next update. Cost: a little overshoot when someone stops or turns. In `RemotePlayer.Tick` and `DragSync.ShowOthers`; no protocol change (snapshots carry positions and their times). Verification: `map4_push` with `DWMP_LAG_MS`, plus a measure of how far each puppet is from the real player.
- **Size:** M.

## Manual verification checklist
